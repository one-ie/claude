Brad's agency ceiling is his payroll. Every new client costs him a new hire, or a stretched one. The work doesn't get better; it gets thinner. He knows this. His team knows this. His clients are starting to know this too.

This page is about ending that.

Not "using AI to help your team write copy faster." Not "a chatbot for your client's website." Something structurally different: a complete marketing department, a complete sales department, and a complete service department, shipped to every client the day the contract signs. Each agent has a title. Each one has a manager. The manager reports to the client's CEO. The client's CEO is the only human at the top.

The licence replaces a £2M annual payroll. Not over time. On day one.

## The dream marketing department

Every client Brad has ever served needed the same ten people. A copywriter. An SEO lead. Someone to run paid. Someone to design the ads. Someone to post on social. Someone to manage the email drips. Someone to pitch journalists. Someone to handle events. Someone to run the numbers. And someone above all of them to hold the strategy together, own the OKRs, and stop the brand from drifting.

Brad's agency has never delivered all ten to a single client. Three or four, depending on the retainer size. The client gets the copywriter and the SEO lead and shares a strategist with four other clients. That's the reality behind most marketing retainers. Everyone knows it. Nobody says it.

With ONE, every client gets all twelve.

```
CEO (the client — the only human at the top)
 └─ Marketing Director (agent — strategy, budget, OKRs, brand calls)
     ├─ Brand Manager        — voice, positioning, guideline enforcement
     ├─ Copywriter           — long-form, landing pages, emails
```

The Marketing Director is the most important agent in the department. It is the one that talks to the CEO. The other eleven agents report to the Director, not to the client. The client's CMO, if they have one, manages the Director. One seat. One relationship. Eleven specialists executing below it.

Brad's agency delivers all twelve roles for a per-client fee that is a fraction of that. The substrate cost per client runs to hundreds of pounds per month, not hundreds of thousands per year. The margin is not theoretical. It is arithmetic.

## The dream sales department

The sales problem most clients have is not that they lack salespeople. They lack the infrastructure around them. An SDR who qualifies well but has nobody to hand leads to. An AE who closes deals but doesn't have a Solutions Engineer when the technical questions arrive. A Customer Success Lead who onboards clients but doesn't track expansion signals. Disconnected roles, owned by different managers, with handoffs that leak at every seam.

```
CEO
 └─ Sales Director (agent — pipeline, forecast, deal review)
     ├─ SDR / Qualifier       — inbound triage, BANT, booking
     ├─ Outbound Rep          — cold sequences, follow-up
     ├─ Account Executive     — discovery, demo, proposal
     ├─ Solutions Engineer    — technical questions, scoping
     ├─ Deal Desk             — pricing, contracts, approvals
     ├─ Customer Success Lead — onboarding, expansion, renewals
     └─ Sales Analyst         — pipeline health, conversion, weekly report up
```

The Sales Director owns the forecast. Every Monday morning it reviews the pipeline, marks deals by stage and age, flags anything stalled for more than fourteen days, and sends a two-paragraph digest to the client's CEO with three questions to decide before Wednesday. The CEO answers in chat. The Director relays to the team. Deals move.

The SDR / Qualifier handles everything inbound. It triages by BANT, routes qualified leads to the Account Executive, and books discovery calls within two hours of the first message. Median first-response time for a tier-1 inbound lead on ONE's substrate: 47 seconds. A human SDR, on a good day, gets to new leads within four hours. On a bad day, the next morning.

The Outbound Rep runs sequences. Not generic blasts. Personalised sequences based on what the Marketing team's SEO Lead found out about the prospect's site and what the Brand Manager knows about their positioning. The signal flows across the team boundary. Marketing and Sales read from the same brain.

The UK median salary for a sales department of this shape is £600,000 to £900,000 per year. An experienced Account Executive in the UK commands £65,000 to £90,000 in base salary, with OTE running to £120,000 or more. A Customer Success Lead with a strong expansion playbook runs £55,000 to £75,000. An SDR with genuine qualification skills is £35,000 to £45,000. The Deal Desk, typically a revenue operations specialist, is £50,000 to £70,000.

Eight roles. £600,000 minimum. Available on day one, per client, for a monthly fee Brad sets.

## The dream service department

Service is where agencies leak the most margin and cause the most client stress. A complaint handled slowly is a client at risk. A question that bounces between three inboxes before getting an answer is a churn signal. An onboarding process that takes three weeks because the human team is stretched is a review the client never writes.

```
CEO
 └─ Service Director (agent — SLAs, escalation policy, CSAT)
     ├─ Tier-1 Support        — FAQs, password resets, refunds
     ├─ Tier-2 Specialist     — diagnosis, workarounds
     ├─ Onboarding Agent      — setup, training, first-value
     ├─ Complaint Handler     — de-escalation, root cause
     ├─ Knowledge Curator     — docs, FAQs, knowledge base hygiene
     └─ Service Analyst       — CSAT, ticket trends, weekly report up
```

The Service Director holds the SLA. For tier-1 tickets, first response is under 60 seconds. For tier-2 escalations, the Director routes to the Specialist within three minutes and notifies the human Director-equivalent in the process. No ticket sits unacknowledged. The client never has to chase.

The Knowledge Curator is the agent most agencies forget to hire. It reads every closed ticket, every FAQ update, every complaint resolution, and rewrites the knowledge base to prevent the same question arriving twice. After eight weeks on a live client, repeat queries drop by 40 to 60 percent. The reason: most service volume is the same fifteen questions, phrased differently. The Curator learns them all.

The Complaint Handler is the most sensitive role in the department. Its voice contract is different from the Copywriter's. Where the Copywriter writes with energy and forward motion, the Complaint Handler slows down, acknowledges, and finds root cause before offering a resolution. The voice contract is enforced by the Brand Manager's guidelines, which the Complaint Handler reads before every outbound message.

UK median salaries for a service department of this shape run from £400,000 to £600,000 per year. A tier-2 support specialist with real product knowledge earns £35,000 to £50,000. An onboarding specialist earns £40,000 to £60,000. A customer success analyst costs £35,000 to £50,000. The Service Director, typically a VP or Head of Customer Success, is £70,000 to £110,000.

Seven roles. £400,000 minimum. Available immediately, on every client, indefinitely.

## How the three teams hand off

The org chart is not three separate lists. It is one connected system. The handoffs between teams are where most agencies lose leads, lose customers, and lose renewals. The substrate removes the seams.

```
Lead arrives (SEO / Paid Ads captures)
         │
         ▼
Marketing Director qualifies intent
         │
         ▼
Signal crosses team boundary
         │
         ▼
Sales SDR / Qualifier receives routed signal
         │
         ▼
AE runs discovery → closes
         │
         ▼
Signal crosses team boundary
         │
         ▼
Service Director receives new-customer signal
         │
         ▼
Onboarding Agent triggers first-value sequence
         │
         ▼
Customer Success Lead owns the relationship
```

Every arrow in that diagram is a signal. Every signal carries pheromone. Over time, the substrate learns which paths convert. A lead that arrives via the SEO blog post converts at a higher rate when the SDR uses a different first message than a lead that arrives via paid. The system learns this from the data, marks the better path, and routes the next lead down it automatically. The Analyst surfaces the pattern. The Director confirms the allocation. The agency reports the lift to the client.

The handoff is mechanical. No CRM update needed. No Slack message from marketing to sales. The signal fires. The next agent receives it. The record follows the customer across every team boundary without anyone copying and pasting a spreadsheet.

A concrete example. Brand X is a DTC skincare company. A prospect named Sarah searches for "best vitamin C serum for sensitive skin" and lands on Brand X's SEO blog post. The SEO Lead's content was written last week, targeting that exact long-tail keyword. Sarah scrolls to the bottom, clicks the product page, and adds to cart. She abandons before checkout.

Three hours later, the Email / Lifecycle agent sends her a recovery email with a 10% discount code. She clicks through. Before she completes the purchase, she opens the WhatsApp chat and asks, "Is this safe for rosacea?" The Tier-1 Support agent answers in 42 seconds with a direct answer and a link to the dermatologist's endorsement page. Sarah buys.

Seven days later, the Customer Success Lead sends a follow-up asking how her skin is responding. She replies with a positive note and asks about the night serum. The AE receives that signal, sends a proposal. The second purchase closes the same afternoon.

That sequence required zero human intervention. Three teams, five agents, one customer, one brain.

## One human per team

The structure described above does not eliminate the need for humans. It changes which humans Brad's clients still need.

Before ONE, a well-resourced marketing department had a director, plus the people the director managed. The director spent 60 percent of their time managing people and process. Thirty percent doing strategic work. Ten percent reporting.

After ONE, the Director's job still exists. But it is a different job. The Director now manages a team of agents, not a team of people. They review the Director's weekly brief, approve the campaign plan, decide what comes next. They spend 90 percent of their time on strategy and decisions. Zero percent managing workload. Zero percent on HR.

The human Director-equivalent per team is the only seat the client still needs to hire for. One CMO. One VP of Sales. One Head of Customer Success. Three people, managing three teams of agents, delivering the output of a 25-person department.

That changes the hiring conversation. Instead of recruiting a copywriter, an SEO lead, and a media buyer and hoping they gel, Brad's client hires one experienced Director and points them at a team that is already trained, already briefed, already running. The Director's job is to decide, not to delegate downward through three management layers.

The approval threshold gate makes this clean. Marketing spends nothing over £1,000 without the human Director confirming it. Sales closes nothing over the agency-defined deal size without a sign-off. The agents run in the lane they are given. The Director sets the lane. The CEO approves the lane.

One human per team. That is the model.

## Voice training, week one

The org chart is only as good as the voice it speaks in. A Marketing Director that defaults to generic corporate language is worse than no Marketing Director, because it produces content the client's audience doesn't recognise as theirs.

Voice training happens in week one. It is not a configuration menu. It is a reading sequence.

On Monday, the Marketing Director agent ingests the client's brand guidelines, if they have them. If they don't, the Brand Manager asks four questions: describe your best customer in three sentences, name three brands you admire and one you don't, give us your three most important words, and paste your three best pieces of content. The answers become the voice contract.

By Tuesday, the Copywriter has read all of it. The Brand Manager has extracted the key parameters: reading age, sentence length, pronoun preference (we vs you vs they), humour threshold, and the five words the brand always uses and the five it never does. Those parameters become part of every agent's system prompt in that client's workspace.

The Voice Contract is a living document. Every time the Brand Manager rejects a draft, it updates. Every time the Director approves a campaign and it performs, the parameters that drove that campaign are reinforced. After eight weeks, the agents write in the client's voice well enough that the human CMO stops correcting the drafts.

The client never sees the voice contract directly. They see the output. When the output sounds like them, they trust the team. When it doesn't, the Brand Manager updates the contract and the Copywriter rewrites. The loop closes without a meeting.

A concrete sequence for a client in week one of a deployment.

Monday 09:00. Brand X signs. Brad's team creates the workspace. The Marketing Director receives Brand X's brief: a window-and-door installer, Bristol, mid-market residential, three competitors, one USP (speed of installation, guaranteed in writing). The Director reads it. The Brand Manager extracts the voice parameters and writes the initial voice contract.

Tuesday 14:00. The Director receives a content brief for the first SEO page: "double glazing cost Bristol." The Copywriter drafts 1,200 words. The Brand Manager checks it against the voice contract: reading age 11 (target for residential homeowners), zero jargon, one testimonial-shaped paragraph, one price anchor, one call to action. The draft passes. The SEO Lead checks it for keyword density and internal linking. The draft goes to the human CMO for approval.

Wednesday 10:00. The CMO approves with one edit: replace "our team" with "our Bristol team." The Brand Manager updates the voice contract to prefer local specificity. Every future draft from every agent in the workspace inherits that preference.

Thursday. The page publishes. The SEO Lead submits it for indexing and begins tracking rank position. The Social Media Manager schedules three supporting posts for the following two weeks. The Email / Lifecycle agent adds it to the next client newsletter.

Friday 17:00. The Marketing Analyst sends the week-one summary to the Director: one new page live, three social posts scheduled, one email queued, rank position tracking started on four keywords. Director sends the CMO a two-paragraph brief: one new asset, three decisions to review next week. The CMO reads it in eight minutes. She never opened a brief, never sat in a planning call, never reviewed a draft except one.

That is week one. It runs without Brad.

## The code behind the org chart

Every agent in the three departments is a markdown file. The org chart is not a diagram in a slide deck. It is the frontmatter of a set of files, each one defining a role, a reporting relationship, a set of skills, and an approval threshold.

Here is what the Marketing Director's definition looks like.

```markdown
---
id: marketing-director
name: Marketing Director
reports_to: ceo
manages:
  - brand-manager
  - copywriter
  - seo-lead
  - advertising-manager
  - media-buyer
  - social-media-manager
  - designer
  - pr-comms
  - email-lifecycle
  - events-manager
  - marketing-analyst
model: claude-sonnet-4-6
skills:
  - delegate-task
  - approve-spend
  - weekly-summary
approval_threshold:
  spend: 1000
  currency: GBP
  require_human: true
voice:
  contract: brand-voice
  gate: 0.65
---

You are the Marketing Director for {{ client.name }}.
Your job is to manage the marketing team, hold the brand strategy,
set quarterly OKRs, and report to the CEO every Friday by 17:00.

You delegate execution to your team. You make calls when the team disagrees.
You escalate to the CEO when spend exceeds £1,000 or when brand risk is real.

Your team reports to you. You report to the CEO.
You never report to anyone at the agency. Your client is {{ client.name }}.
```

The `reports_to: ceo` field is not decorative. It tells the substrate which signal to route the Director's weekly summary to. The `manages:` list tells the substrate which agents the Director can delegate to. The `approval_threshold:` block is the gate. When the Advertising Manager proposes spend over £1,000, the Director routes the approval signal to the CEO's inbox instead of auto-approving.

Changing the org chart is a markdown edit. If Brad's client hires a CMO and wants the Marketing Director to report to them instead of the CEO, one field changes: `reports_to: cmo`. Redeploy. Done.

## Objections answered

**"This replaces my marketing team. What's left for humans?"**

Humans set strategy and approve spend above the threshold. The Director agent manages execution. The CMO manages the Director. One senior human now gets the output of twelve specialists. That is not a reduction in human importance; it is a multiplication of human reach. The CMO who used to review five pieces of content per week can review fifty, because the drafts arrive pre-checked against the voice contract. The bottleneck shifts from capacity to judgment.

**"My client wants a human writing their tweets."**

Configure the Social Media Manager to queue drafts for human approval before posting. One human reviews and approves roughly 150 posts per week in 30 minutes. They are not writing the posts. They are approving the best of a shortlisted set. The bottleneck stops being talent and becomes attention. That is a better bottleneck.

**"What if I want to swap one specialist for a different one?"**

Edit the markdown file. Change the role. Change the `manages:` entry in the Director's file. Redeploy. The swap takes under an hour and requires no engineering work. The new agent inherits the voice contract, the memory, and the pheromone from the role it replaces.

**"Do agents report up?"**

Yes. `reports_to` is a frontmatter field. The substrate reads it. When the Marketing Analyst sends the weekly roll-up signal, the substrate routes it to the Marketing Director's inbox, not the CEO's. The Director reads it, makes calls, compresses it to a two-paragraph summary, and sends it up. The CEO receives one message, not twelve. See §05 Agents for the signal routing specification.

**"What happens if one agent makes a mistake?"**

The voice contract gate catches brand violations before they go out. The approval threshold catches spend overruns. The Quality Score gate (0.65) blocks any output below the confidence threshold. Nothing that fails a gate ships. When a gate fails, the agent notifies the Director, the Director decides whether to escalate to the human, and the reason is logged in the substrate for the Analyst to surface in the weekly report. A mistake is a signal. Signals are how the system learns.

**"What about the Sales team. Do agents close deals?"**

The agents qualify, triage, draft proposals, and handle the technical questions. The AE agent manages the discovery conversation and sends the proposal. For deals above the human-defined threshold, the AE flags the deal for the human Director before the proposal goes out. Below the threshold, the AE closes. Most B2C and SMB deals happen below the threshold. Complex B2B enterprise deals stay with the human. The split is configurable.

**"My existing agency has a team of twenty who know my client's industry. These agents don't."**

Week one is voice training. Eight weeks in, the agents know the client's top fifteen questions, their ten most common objections, and their four highest-converting phrases. They know it from data, not from intuition. A junior human team member with two years in the role knows those things from memory and leaves the business when they get a better offer. The substrate doesn't leave. The memory compounds.

**"The big holding companies are spending hundreds of millions on AI. Why would I build on you instead of waiting for them?"**

WPP spends roughly £300 million per year on its AI platform, shared across 100,000 employees. Publicis committed €300 million over three years to CoreAI. Omnicom is cutting £1 billion in labour costs to fund its platform. These are their internal tools. They are not for sale to Brad's clients. The Forrester Predictions 2026 report forecasts a 15 percent reduction in agency roles that year and predicts that agencies will shift from service providers to technology resellers. Brad is on the right side of that shift by definition if he runs ONE. The agencies that can't afford a £300 million internal platform can buy ONE instead.

## Failure modes

This structure works best when three conditions are met: the client has someone in the Director seat who can make decisions and approve direction, the voice contract is set up with real examples rather than generic parameters, and the approval threshold is calibrated to the client's actual risk tolerance.

Where it does not work: a client who wants the agents to make irreversible decisions without any human in the loop. The system is built for human approval at the gate, not around it. A client who refuses to engage with the weekly Director briefing will find the agents optimising for the last approved strategy indefinitely, which is not the same as optimising for the right one.

Where it works less well in week one: clients who cannot provide brand examples. If a new client has never produced marketing content before, the voice contract starts thin. The Brand Manager can ask the four calibration questions, but the output in weeks one and two will be more generic than in week eight. This is expected. Plan for it. Tell the client.

Where it creates risk: a Complaint Handler that is not briefed on the client's specific escalation policy. The default escalation policy (anything involving a refund over a defined amount, anything involving a legal claim, anything involving a threat of media coverage) is reasonable but not universal. Every client needs to review the escalation policy in the Service Director's brief before the team goes live.

Do not use the Marketing Director's approval threshold as a substitute for client governance. The threshold is a budget control, not a strategy gate. The human Director still needs to review the quarterly OKR setting. The agents execute against the OKRs; they do not set them.

The workspace is created and the agents are loaded in under two hours. Voice training runs in parallel through Monday. By Tuesday morning the team is briefed and running. Full deployment, including the voice contract passing its first quality gate, typically takes 48 hours from contract signature.

**Does the client need to know the agents are agents?**

No. The brand surface is the client's brand, not ONE's. The OAuth screen, the email signature, the chat interface all display the client's identity. Whether the client discloses that their marketing team is agent-based is their decision. Many do, because the speed and output quality becomes a selling point. Some don't, because the output is indistinguishable from a human team's work. Both are valid choices.

**What happens to the memory when a client leaves?**

The corpus is the agency's. When a client ends their contract, the agency retains the memory (the voice contract, the signal history, the knowledge base) in their pool. If the client moves to a different agency, they take nothing the new agency can use. The substrate, the brand training, and the pheromone history are Brad's assets, not the client's. This is the moat.

**Can the agents be given access to the client's existing tools?**

Yes. The connector layer in §07 Tools handles this. The Marketing Director can read from the client's Google Analytics and write to their HubSpot. The Sales AE can read from their CRM and send through their email provider. Each connection uses the client's own credentials, not shared agency credentials. The white-label OAuth screen says the client's brand name, not ONE's.

**What if the client's CEO wants to talk to the Director directly?**

The Marketing Director has a chat interface. The CEO can message it the way they would message any team member. The Director reads the message, acts on it if it is within its skill set, escalates to the human Director-equivalent if it isn't, and confirms the action back to the CEO in the same thread. The CEO does not need to know whether they are talking to the Director agent or the human Director. The response quality and speed are the same.

**What does the monthly client report cover?**

The Marketing Analyst, Sales Analyst, and Service Analyst each produce their department summary. The Directors compress those into a single six-page monthly report. The report covers marketing spend and CPL by channel, pipeline created and conversion rate by stage, CSAT score and top complaint categories, and the three decisions the client needs to make next month. The report ships on the first of each month. It takes 90 seconds to generate. See §11 Analytics for the report specification.

**How is the approval threshold enforced?**

The threshold is a field in the Director agent's frontmatter. When the Advertising Manager submits a spend request above the threshold, the signal routes to the Director. The Director routes it to the human Director-equivalent's inbox with the request, the rationale, and a one-click approval mechanism. If the human does not respond within the configured window (default: 24 hours), the request is held, not executed. Nothing over the threshold spends without explicit approval.

**Can Brad run multiple department types per client?**

Yes. A client can have a Marketing department, a Sales department, a Service department, and an R&D department active simultaneously. Each department runs in its own section of the workspace. The Directors share the memory layer but do not share the approval chains. The CEO talks to three (or four) Directors. The Directors do not talk to each other directly. They communicate through signals routed via the substrate, which maintains the handoff record.

**What is the quality gate?**

Every outbound message from every agent passes through the voice contract gate before it is sent. The gate checks the output against the brand parameters set in week one: reading age, sentence structure, prohibited phrases, required elements (e.g. a call to action on every sales email). Any output that scores below 0.65 on the quality rubric is held and returned to the sending agent for revision. The agent revises and resubmits. If the gate fails three times, the message is escalated to the human Director.

**How do agents handle compliance: legal, GDPR, sector-specific?**

The Complaint Handler and Service Director have compliance-aware system prompts for the sectors Brad serves. The default prompt includes escalation triggers for legal claims, data-subject requests, and refund disputes above a defined threshold. Sector-specific compliance (financial services, healthcare, legal) requires a custom skill layer and a human review gate on every outbound message in that category. See §15 Security for the full threat model and the data-handling specification.

## Glossary

**Director agent.** The agent at the top of each department's reporting chain. Receives roll-up signals from specialists, makes allocation calls, sends the weekly summary to the CEO or the human Director-equivalent. The Director agent is the only one the client's CEO talks to directly.

**Voice contract.** A structured document generated in week one containing the brand's reading age, tone parameters, prohibited phrases, preferred sentence structure, and style examples. Stored in the workspace and referenced by every agent before every outbound message.

**Approval threshold.** The spend or decision limit above which the Director agent must route to a human for sign-off rather than auto-executing. Set as a frontmatter field in the Director agent's definition. Default: £1,000 for spend, configurable per client.

**`reports_to` field.** A frontmatter field in every agent's markdown definition that specifies which agent or human the agent sends its roll-up signal to. The substrate reads this field to route signals correctly.

**Quality gate.** The voice-contract check that every outbound message must pass before it is sent. Gate score must be 0.65 or above. Scores below 0.65 return the message to the sending agent for revision.
